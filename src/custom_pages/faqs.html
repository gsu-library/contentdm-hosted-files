Frequently Asked Questions

Information About What Digital Collections Is:

Are the images here considered primary resources?

Yes! The materials within Digital Collections are digital copies of some of the archival material within Special Collections and Archives.

Are these items different from what is in Special Collections?

No. These items are digital copies of a portion of what is held in the Special Collections and Archives Department within the University Library. Some items within the collection are unique as they are natively digital, so the best place to see them is digital collections.

How is material selected for digitization?

Each curatorial area Archivist has a list of material they would like to have digitized. They create this list based on user requests, analytics, current events, scholarly importance and/or discussions with donors. Once priorities are set within Special Collections and Archives, conversations are held with Digital Projects to determine workload and timing. Material is also selected based on requests for high-resolution copies. If there are no copyright or privacy concerns, that material will be included in Digital Collections as well.

Can I suggest material for digitization?

You cannot directly suggest material for digitization to Digital Projects. You can reach out to the Special Collections and Archives and suggest they add it to their digital projects list. This does not guarantee your suggestion will be chosen. If you only need a few images from a collection, the Archivists may be able to help you get them without waiting for Digital Projects to add them to our queue. To learn more about the process visit Special Collections and Archives services page: https://library.gsu.edu/special-collections/services/

Can I donate material for inclusion into Digital Collections?

You cannot donate material directly to Digital Projects for inclusion into Digital Collections. Donations are handled by Special Collections and Archives. During the donation process, we will discuss if the collection is appropriate for Digital Collections and any relevant timelines.

How often is new material added to the Digital Collections?

New material is added monthly to Digital Collections. We currently do not have a way of notifying the public of what has been added. If you have any questions about what has been added, please contact digitalcollections@gsu.edu.

I own the copyright to an item within digital collections and want it removed. What do I do?

Requests for removal should be directed to archives@gsu.edu or (404) 413-2880 Your inquiry should include the name and URL of the item or items to be removed, the reason for the removal request, and an explanation of any claims of rights to the material. For more information about Georgia State University Library’s takedown procedure, please visit: https://digitalcollections.library.gsu.edu/digital/custom/copyright

I can’t find any theses. Where are they located?

Theses have their own repository which can be found here: https://scholarworks.gsu.edu/

What if I think your metadata is inaccurate? How should I contact you?

Thanks for looking out for us! Sometimes errors happen, and we appreciate any help in fixing them. Please contact us at digitalcollections@gsu.edu with the URL of the item, the information in question, and any information you have to help us determine the correct information.

I’m having trouble using your site. Who should I contact?

We’ve created a help page with tips on how to use digital collections. It can be found here: https://digitalcollections.library.gsu.edu/digital/custom/help. If you still are having trouble after following those instructions, email digitalcollections@gsu.edu. A staff member from Digital Projects will be able to help you. If you have a suggestion for how to make our site better, please use our contact form, and we will take it into consideration.

Information About Using Archival Material Within Digital Collections:

How can I use these materials?

Material within Digital Collections has been made available to support research and educational purposes to students, researchers and the general public. Not for profit uses do not require permission for use from Special Collections and Archives though there are collection exceptions. Students can use this material for their class research projects and papers without needing to obtain permission from the Library. If an image from Digital Collections is going to be published in an article, journal, book, or any other for-profit purpose, please contact archives@gsu.edu for more information. Regardless of the kind of use, we still require you cite us as your source.

How do I obtain a high-resolution file for an image?

Please contact archives@gsu.edu, and they will be able to help you. To learn more about the process visit Special Collections and Archives services page: https://library.gsu.edu/special-collections/services/

Who do I contact if I want to publish an item within Digital Collections?

Please contact archives@gsu.edu if you want to publish an item from Digital Collections. Fees may apply. To learn more about the process visit Special Collections and Archives services page: https://library.gsu.edu/special-collections/services/

Do you know who the copyright holder is for items that I want to publish?

Digital Projects is in the process of providing more copyright information to help researchers determine who the copyright holder is for some items. In some cases, copyright has been transferred to Georgia State University. Otherwise, the Archivist responsible for the collection may have more information to help you determine the copyright holder or who to contact.

Do I need permission to use an image on social media?

Generally no, but we ask you to please tag @gsu_archives to credit where you found the image. If you would like to use an Atlanta Journal-Constitution photo, please reach out to archives@gsu.edu on how to obtain permission.

How do I cite an item within Digital Collections?

Citations can be found in the metadata records within Digital Collections. They follow the following format: Item Title or Item Identifier, Archival Collection, Curatorial Area, Special Collections and Archives, Georgia State University.
