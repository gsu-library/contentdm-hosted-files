Accessibility

Digital Collections is a digital archive which primarily hosts digital surrogates of archival materials held in Special Collections and Archives. We are committed to integrating digital accessibility practices into our procedures to improve digital accessibility for people with disabilities.

Website Compliance

Digital Collections uses OCLC’s CONTENTdm which is compliant with WCAG 2.2 AA guidelines. Information about their compliance can be requested from OCLC.

Content Compliance

Majority of the content held within Digital Collections are exempt from Title II for the following reasons:

  1. “The content was created before the date the state or local government must comply with this rule, or reproduces paper documents or the contents of other physical media (audiotapes, film negatives, and CD-ROMs for example) that were created before the government must comply with this rule, AND”

    The original creation dates (not digitization date) were prior to April 2026.

  2. “The content is kept only for reference, research, or recordkeeping, AND”

    Per the about page on Digital Collections website, the files are made available for research and reference purposes.

  3. “The content is kept in a special area for archived content, AND”

    Per the about page on Digital Collections website, the websites services as an online archive.

  4. “The content has not been changed since it was archived.”

    The digital surrogates are a 1 to 1 reflection of the physical materials and will not be edited, changed, or modified.

Source: https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/

Future Plans

While most of the content within Digital Collections is exempt from Title II regulations, we are making efforts to make content as accessible as possible given our resources. Going forward:

Textual Documents

For newly digitized documents, Digital Projects will run Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and the Adobe Accessibility Checker on all PDFs generated. The OCR will also be made available for viewing in the metadata record.

Image Based Files

For newly digitized photographs, Digital Projects will ensure that all images have a description and alt-text when appropriate. If text is present in the image, we will ensure it documented in the description. Digital Projects plans to retroactively update descriptions and include alt-text in our metadata. This will be a multi-year effort.

Audio-Visual Files

For new Audio-visual items, Digital Projects will have a transcript associated with the record, and captions uploaded to YouTube. Digital Projects plans to retroactively reprocess currently available transcripts for easier to access. We also plan to convert those transcripts to caption files for upload into YouTube for further accessibility. This will be a multi-year effort.

Born Digital Files

Digital Projects and Special Collections and Archives will assess born Digital Files to determine their exemption status. For image and AV based materials, we will follow procedures for digitized materials. For text-based documents, we will access the accessibility status of each document and determine if any intervention is necessary.

Accessibility Requests

If someone requires further accessibility remediation to use the material, please email digitalcollections@gsu.edu with links to the records needed. We will discuss the item, create a plan, and timeline for your request with you.
